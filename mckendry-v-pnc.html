McKendry v. PNC Bank, N.A.

What the Case Is About

McKendry v. PNC Bank, N.A. is a federal civil rights case alleging violations of ADA Title III, the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, and the E-SIGN Act, arising from disability discrimination in bank onboarding. The plaintiff — the founder of this site — is an SSA-adjudicated disabled individual litigating the case without a lawyer.

The core allegations, as published across this site's materials:

The Onboarding Form

The complaint alleges that the bank's digital account-onboarding form requires every customer to select an employment classification from a closed list that contains no accurate option for a recipient of Social Security Disability Insurance — forcing disabled customers to misclassify themselves in order to complete the process.

The Adverse Action

The complaint alleges that the bank closed the plaintiff's account, an adverse action under ECOA's implementing regulation, triggering the statute's written-notice requirements.

The Written Admission

As noted in this site's press kit, the case record includes a written admission from the defendant.

Status: the case is pending. Everything above is an allegation from an active lawsuit, not an adjudicated finding — no court has ruled on the merits. The Legal Framework

The statutes and standards the case moves through — the same authorities players work with in the Record Forge module:

ADA Title III — Public Accommodations
42 U.S.C. § 12181 et seq.

Prohibits disability discrimination at places of public accommodation — a category that expressly includes banks, § 12181(7)(F). The screen-out rule, § 12182(b)(2)(A)(i), bars eligibility criteria that screen out or tend to screen out individuals with disabilities; § 12182(b)(2)(A)(ii) requires reasonable modification of policies, practices, and procedures.

ECOA — Discrimination Prohibitions
15 U.S.C. § 1691(a)

Prohibits discrimination against any applicant because all or part of the applicant's income derives from a public assistance program, § 1691(a)(2), and prohibits adverse action against a person for exercising any right under the Consumer Credit Protection Act, § 1691(a)(3).

ECOA — Adverse Action Notice
15 U.S.C. § 1691(d) · 12 C.F.R. § 1002.9 (Regulation B)

Requires written notice of adverse action within 30 days, stating specific reasons for the action or disclosing the applicant's right to request them. Account closure constitutes adverse action under 12 C.F.R. § 1002.2(c).

E-SIGN Act
15 U.S.C. § 7001 et seq.

Governs the use of electronic records and signatures in consumer transactions, including the consent and disclosure conditions under which an electronic record may substitute for a written one.

Pro Se Standard
Haines v. Kerner, 404 U.S. 519 (1972)

Pro se pleadings are held to less stringent standards than formal pleadings drafted by lawyers. The controlling standard for how courts must read filings by litigants without counsel.
