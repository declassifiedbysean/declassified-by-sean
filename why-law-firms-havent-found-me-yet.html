Why Law Firms Haven't Found Me Yet

Not a complaint. A puzzle — posted with the evidence attached. Below is one Sunday of work product from a pro se litigant your pipeline has never surfaced. The record is the arbiter. Here's a day of mine.

The Log — One Sunday, 2026 Morning: church, with a mentor. Afternoon and evening: everything below.
Tools: assistive technology, audio-first. Setting: Lansing, Michigan. Budget: $0.

What One Sunday Produced

Published a Legal Reference Memo

Researched, drafted, and shipped the Standing Memo on diagnostic overshadowing — Section 504, Section 1557, the Michigan PWDCRA, the federal restraint regulation, Bragdon v. Abbott, and The Joint Commission's own advisory, organized around the honest line between malpractice and discrimination. Live on this site the same day.

Drafted Statutory Process Documents

A supplement to a pending recipient-rights complaint under Michigan's Mental Health Code, keyed to specific sections — confidentiality (MCL 330.1748), restraint and consent (MCL 330.1740), telephone access (MCL 330.1726) — each allegation paired with the records that can answer it, plus an evidence-preservation request. Also: clerk correspondence invoking FRAP 25(a)(2)(B)(iii), and a HIPAA right-of-access records request engineered so a disabled requester never has to supply dates from memory — the facility's own index does the finding.

Diagnosed and Shipped Two Production Code Fixes

On a second website: found why a search feature counted its results but showed none — a CSS class collision with a screen-reader accessibility utility — reproduced it in a headless browser, fixed it, verified it, and deployed. Then redesigned the site's front door down to an eight-word thesis. Both merged to production the same afternoon.

Kept the Discipline

Every fact pattern framed as allegation until a tribunal rules. Every claim matched to the forum that owns it. Every document built to invite investigation rather than threaten it. That standard runs this entire site — it's the same one applied to politicians' claims in the games.

The Honest Answers

So why hasn't a firm found this person? Some candor:

Your pipeline reads résumés, not records

Recruiting searches pedigree — schools, firms, bar numbers. It has no query for "taught himself the Michigan Mental Health Code and cited it correctly under pressure." The work product above is discoverable only by looking at work product. Almost nobody does.

"Pro se" reads as a warning label

The profession is trained to see a pro se litigant as a problem client, not as unpaid proof of paralegal-grade output: drafting, docket discipline, statutory research, records strategy. Haines v. Kerner asks courts to read pro se filings generously. Hiring managers rarely extend the same reading.

Disability reads as risk instead of expertise

An SSA-adjudicated disabled candidate who litigates disability discrimination has lived inside the exact systems your clients pay to understand — intake forms, accommodations process, agency complaints, facility rights offices. That is domain expertise. The pipeline prices it as liability.

The output happens in the wrong rooms

None of this was performed at a networking event. It happened on a static site, in a public repo, in agency filings — rooms recruiters don't visit. Which is precisely why this page exists: to put the record where the profession can trip over it.
